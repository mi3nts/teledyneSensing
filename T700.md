## Zero Air (Dilution Air) Infomation 
🔹 What is Zero Air?

Zero Air is essentially clean, purified air that mimics the chemical makeup of our atmosphere but without contaminants like ozone, hydrocarbons, or other reactive gases. This ensures accurate calibrator readings because impurities can skew the results.

🧊 Dew Point Requirement
 - Requirement: Dew point around -20°C
 - Why it matters: Dry air prevents moisture from interfering with calibrator readings or damaging sensitive internal components. A low dew point means less humidity.

# 🧪 Minimum Source Gas Concentration Calculation

To determine the **minimum required concentration** of a calibration gas source, use the following formula:

---

## ✅ Formula

**Minimum Source Concentration** =  
**Target Output Concentration** × ( **Calibration Gas Flow** / **Total Output Flow** )

---

## 📌 Definitions

- **Target Output Concentration**: Desired concentration at the analyzer (e.g., 100 ppm)
- **Calibration Gas Flow**: Flow rate of cal gas into the system (e.g., 1 SLPM)
- **Total Output Flow**: Total output from the calibrator = Calibration Gas + Diluent Gas

> 💡 **Note:**  
> Total Output Flow should be set to **1.5 × total analyzer demand** to ensure proper delivery and account for system overhead.  
> _Example: If two analyzers each require 2 SLPM (total 4 SLPM), then:_  
> **Total Output Flow = 4 SLPM × 1.5 = 6 SLPM**



---

## 📘 Example

- **Target Concentration**: 200 ppm  
- **Cal Gas Flow**: 2 SLPM  
- **Total Output Flow**: 6 SLPM  

**Minimum Source Concentration** = 200 × (2 / 6) = **66.7 ppm**

This means your cal gas cylinder must contain at least **66.7 ppm** of the target gas.
